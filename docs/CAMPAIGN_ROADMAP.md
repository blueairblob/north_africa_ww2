# North African campaign scope

**Owner direction, 1 November 2026:** Benghazi Handicap should grow from its Crusader opening into a playable North African campaign. The target already stated in `DESIGN.md` is September 1940 through November 1942. Individual battles remain available as independent starts; the full campaign connects them. The earlier game's broad chronological scope is inspiration for this structure, not a source for scenario data, rules or wording (`CLEAN_ROOM.md`).

The current PC game and its map are the development platform for this work. A future phone or small-map version is a separate design question and must not constrain the campaign scope.

## Player experience

- **Battle:** choose a dated operation, its side, opponent and strategic assumptions. It starts from a researched historical snapshot and ends with an assessment of its High Command orders.
- **Campaign:** start in 1940, issue orders through the changing war, and carry losses, control, stocks, fortifications and formation history forward. At a chapter boundary, show the debrief and the next High Command orders, then continue from the *actual* state of the game. A separate historical start remains available for each chapter.
- **Operational pauses:** represent long quiet intervals by dated strategic turns or an explicit, player-approved advance that resolves supply, repair, replacements, withdrawal and preparation. Do not silently replace the player's outcome with the historical one.

The trial now has a bounded F6/Game-menu advance for a single human commander. It resolves at most five normal turns, autosaves each one and stops for visible contact, field events, changed place control, mission progress, an ended standing order or changes to a friendly formation's strength, supply trace, stores or status. The player must inspect the position and explicitly advance again. This reduces empty clicks; the dated bridge now runs from 1 September 1940 through 30 November 1942 and has no coarser strategic-turn model.
- **What-if:** strategic assumptions such as Axis sea delivery remain independent of battle selection. A fuller Malta branch needs sourced changes to shipping and air operations, with a date and consequences; the current ±15-point sea control is only an experimental sensitivity setting.

The campaign is intended to be a genuine continuous game. Chapters are useful entry points and debriefs, not a requirement that history repeats itself. The engine accepts one-day and two-day chapters; battle intervals generally use daily turns. The five-turn advance makes quiet periods playable but still requires repeated review. A player-selectable resolution would change movement, combat opportunities, weather and supply, so it needs separate balance and continuity checks before release.

### Open campaign principle

Historical dates set the opening situation and introduce events that would occur independently of the local battle: reinforcements, withdrawals, command changes, weather and supply conditions. They do **not** trigger a mandatory capture, retreat, siege or named battle. A chapter's title identifies the historical period; the same dates may produce a different operation in a played campaign. High Command states operational aims plainly, such as hold Tobruk or keep the frontier road open. If the played situation makes an order obsolete or impossible, the next briefing must respond to that situation rather than repeat the historical script. The game judges orders against what units actually did and what ground they actually hold.

A future **unit-level AI** may give each formation limited initiative under its commander's intent: choose a route, avoid an exposed crossing, rest when exhausted, exploit a visible opening or withdraw locally when badly threatened. Bounded, seeded variation between sound choices can make matches less predictable. Supply, morale, visibility and command range constrain those choices; a player's explicit order remains the governing intent. Record the random seed and choices for reproducible saves, replays and computer matches. Prototype this separately from the current side-wide computer opponent, then test that formations do not abandon mission ground or create implausible independent raids.

Optional High Command commendations now provide a small reward for an attainable extra order without changing victory. They appear with the mission, are checked at the chapter debrief and persist in the campaign's result history. The April 1941 trial recognises preservation of part of the 9th Australian infantry. This is the first narrow step toward an open campaign with optional missions; it is not a personal medal or a complete quest system.

## Engine and data work

1. **Campaign definition:** add an original, versioned data file for campaign bounds, chapters, strategic events and the conditions for ending a chapter. Keep the existing scenario JSON format and standalone starts valid.
2. **Dated map state:** the current map is one static 1941 interpretation. Audit road and track links, railheads, ports, escarpment passes and any minefield or fortification changes for 1940 and 1942. Use date-aware overlays where needed, without duplicating the entire map for each chapter.
3. **Historical snapshots:** research each independent chapter's opening units, strengths, locations, stocks, owners, HQ structure, arrivals, withdrawals and mission orders. Record uncertainty explicitly and run the scenario checker. Avoid deriving a purported historical start from an earlier game or from an arbitrary turn of our Crusader scenario.
4. **Continuity:** define a transition that retains the played state, applies only dated reinforcements, withdrawals, repairs and strategic events, and issues context-sensitive High Command orders. Resolve absent, destroyed or unexpectedly placed units without resurrecting them. Validate stock and unit invariants after every transition.
5. **Victory:** grade both the chapter's explicit orders and the longer campaign's changing strategic aims. A captured port, relieved siege or blocked road must mean what the order says in the played state. Preserve the distinction between a historical standalone start and an alternate campaign outcome.
6. **Save and playback:** store campaign identity, current chapter, strategic choices and transition history beside the existing session. Load old standalone saves unchanged. Ensure autosaves do not overwrite another campaign or what-if variant.
7. **Computer opponent and usability:** plan over more than one battle, preserve forces through quiet periods, and avoid 400 turns of meaningless orders. Add a campaign overview and chapter debrief; keep the battle setup simple. Explore bounded unit initiative under player or computer orders, with seeded replayable variation.

## Build order and acceptance

Start with one **vertical slice**: a researched earlier chapter (Compass is the leading candidate), a researched transition into a second chapter, and a standalone historical start for each. A campaign begun in the first must carry its real casualties, place control and stocks into the second; the second's historical start must remain independent. Save/load and computer-versus-computer continuation must produce the same result as uninterrupted play. Only then extend the chronology in both directions and offer a whole-war start. A chapter is ready for external review when its opening evidence, mission semantics, supply path, outcomes and at least one plausible win for each side have been checked.

The official-source and map audit for the Compass opening is recorded in [COMPASS_OPENING_RESEARCH.md](COMPASS_OPENING_RESEARCH.md). A daily trial scenario reuses the existing map, with Tummar abstracted as a camp sector. One Hard computer match reached Sidi Barrani on 11 December, a day after the historical town capture. The Indian withdrawal is dated 14 December. A reproducible opening-turn trace now identifies the west-of-Nibeiwa crossing, Axis response, successive assaults and tank serviceability as a connected validation task. The 1940 supply quantities and detailed battle sequence remain unverified, so Compass is explicitly labelled a research trial rather than a normal reviewed battle.

The main menu offers **Campaign research trial**, a linked, saveable 33-chapter run from 1 September 1940 to 30 November 1942. It covers the Italian advance, Compass, the 1941 Cyrenaica and Tobruk campaigns, Crusader, the Axis winter return, Gazala, both Alamein battles and the November pursuit. Battles use daily turns; long preparation periods use two-day turns. A final strategic assessment reports actual holdings and cumulative mission progress. The source and uncertainty audit is in [FULL_CAMPAIGN_TRIAL_1940_1942.md](FULL_CAMPAIGN_TRIAL_1940_1942.md). This is structurally playable, not a validated historical simulation: exact strengths, arrival dates, map sectors, tank serviceability, mines, surrender and supply remain under review. The independent historical Crusader and Sidi Rezegh starts remain available.

The [May frontier research note](FRONTIER_1941_RESEARCH.md) records the sourced Brevity orders and tank figures, the estimated entry dates and manpower, and the missing transport and anti-tank mechanics. The 5–14 May preparation and 15–17 May probe are linked trial chapters. They keep a counterfactual played front line instead of applying the historical withdrawal or possession of Halfaya Pass.

The [Battleaxe research note](BATTLEAXE_1941_RESEARCH.md) records the 26–27 May Halfaya action, the 28 May operational orders, the delayed *Tiger* re-equipment and the 15–17 June trial. Its new strengths and placements remain estimates, and a real refit and serviceability model is still needed.

The [June–December 1941 research note](TOBRUK_PREPARATION_1941_RESEARCH.md) identifies dated garrison relief, 22nd Armoured Brigade arrival, repair and training constraints, and conditional events needed for counterfactual Tobruk holdings. A linked 18 June–31 July two-day-turn chapter preserves the played Battleaxe state. A second chapter covers 1 August–29 September and conditionally relieves the 18th Australian Brigade with the Polish Carpathian Brigade. A daily 30 September–17 November chapter models the 70th Division rotation, the surviving 2/13th Australian Battalion and the 22nd Armoured Brigade's estimated availability. A campaign-only 18 November–31 December Crusader chapter carries that played state and adds missing formations as estimated first-turn entries. Workshop refit, the exact relief sequence and unit-level Crusader historical validation remain to be modelled.

The [Cyrenaica winter research note](CYRENAICA_WINTER_1942_RESEARCH.md) covers a linked 1–20 January consolidation and a daily 21 January–8 February Axis offensive trial. It preserves the played line rather than awarding the historical Axis captures, and conditionally evacuates the 2/13th Australian Battalion on 10 December in the preceding Crusader chapter. The winter roster, serviceability, shipping and Gazala defensive works still need a source-led pass.

The versioned chapter catalog is `data/campaigns/north_africa.json`; the normal battle list contains Crusader and Sidi Rezegh, and the **Campaign research trial** menu opens the 33-chapter September 1940–November 1942 chain. `engine/campaign.py` carries the played state into each contiguous chapter. A human `Session` can continue with its player choices, custom unit names and previous orders in the save history. Destroyed and withdrawn formations remain archived; active and pending formations retain their IDs. Each result offers continuation where the catalog names an adjacent chapter. The normal **New campaign** path remains gated on historical validation. The research trial is playable end to end but its force estimates and rules need source-led review.

The current Crusader and Sidi Rezegh scenarios are playable but their deployment, combat and win balance still have known uncertainties. They do not yet meet the historical acceptance bar for a campaign transition.

## End-to-end delivery sequence

The target is an actual playable, saveable September 1940–November 1942 game, not a menu of disconnected historical battles. These are the remaining delivery gates, in dependency order:

1. **Make the first linked campaign credible.** Verify the Bardia and Tobruk perimeter maps, opposing strengths, engineer breaches, bombardment, tank serviceability, supply and surrender behaviour. Test human and computer wins from plausible played states. The current thirty-three-chapter research chain is structurally continuous but fails this gate.
2. **Verify Compass forward and backward.** Check the linked Derna and Beda Fomm pursuit and the newly drafted September–December Italian invasion and frontier chapters against source orders of battle, positions and readiness. Every day is covered by a dated turn; no chapter resets control, units, losses or stocks to the historical outcome.
3. **Build the 1941 bridge.** Validate the linked Axis arrival, Cyrenaica reversal, Tobruk siege, Brevity and Battleaxe research chapters; then build the quiet preparation before Crusader. Introduce actual dated reinforcements, withdrawals and supply constraints. A long interval needs a strategic-turn resolver that gives both sides decisions without requiring hundreds of empty End Turn clicks.
4. **Validate linked Crusader against played history.** The existing Crusader scenario remains an independent historical start. Its campaign-only trial now inherits the 1941 game's actual position and roster; validate the newly added formations, serviceability and the resulting battles against official accounts and counterfactual tests.
5. **Validate 1942 and the campaign result.** The Axis return, Gazala, First Alamein, Alam Halfa, Second Alamein and November pursuit now link as research chapters. Verify 1942 formations, minefields, repair, fuel, supply, outcomes and the final strategic assessment against official accounts and human play.
6. **Release gate.** A human can start at the campaign's first date, save and resume at any chapter, reach its last date, review a comprehensible final result, and play against a computer opponent. Run deterministic complete-campaign traces and human reviews for both sides; document every historical estimate and known limitation. Only then enable New campaign as the full game.

The next implementation slice is the Bardia/Tobruk fortress and pursuit model. The Australian official history [*To Benghazi*, chapter 9](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Australia/Bengazi/Benghazi-9.html) describes the 6th Division's move and Tobruk preparation; [chapter 10](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Australia/Bengazi/Benghazi-10.html) records the advance to Derna and its 30 January occupation; [chapter 11](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Australia/Bengazi/Benghazi-11.html) covers the Beda Fomm road block and Benghazi. These sources establish chronology and broad forces, not our game-unit strengths or exact 10 km positions. The new pursuit chapters do not yet model the historical mass surrender or the transport shortage that forced the Australians to lift one brigade at a time.
