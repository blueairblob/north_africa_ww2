# Compass opening: research and trial gate

**Status, 5 November 2026:** `data/scenario_drafts/compass_opening.json` is an eight-day **research draft** on the existing 10 km map. Each turn covers one day. It remains outside `data/scenarios/`, so it is absent from the normal battle menu and campaign catalog. A human reviewer can launch it with `python -m screen --trial compass_opening --axis computer-hard`, or double-click `Compass Research Trial.cmd` in an extracted Windows review build. F5 saves and F12 captures the trial, and its embedded scenario can be loaded later. Its unit positions, stocks and combat sequence still need historical validation.

When this opt-in trial reaches its result, **Continue campaign** leads to the
linked 17 December–2 January research interval, then to the 3–5 January
Bardia assault. The continuing game keeps its played units, losses, stocks
and ownership; the interval's daily arrivals, Sollum seahead and assault
limits are described in `docs/BARDIA_OPENING_RESEARCH.md`. This is still an
experimental path outside the standard New campaign menu.

## Sources and firm chronology

- [Wavell's Western Desert operations despatch, London Gazette 37628](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/LondonGazette/37628.pdf), sections 10–21, gives the staged plan, a Western Desert Force aggregate of roughly 31,000 men, 120 guns, 275 tanks and 60 armoured cars, the roughly 50 infantry tanks of 7th RTR before the opening, the forward dumps, and the opening actions. These are **force totals**, not individual counter strengths.
- [UK official history, *The Mediterranean and Middle East*, volume I, chapter XIV](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-I/UK-Med-I-14.html), places the Maletti Group at Nibeiwa, 2nd Libyan Division at Tummar, 1st Libyan Division at Maktila, 63rd Cirene at Rabia/Sofafi, 62nd Marmarica toward Halfaya, 64th Catanzaro near Buq Buq, and 4th Blackshirt Division around Sidi Barrani. It describes 11th Indian Brigade and 7th RTR attacking Nibeiwa on 9 December, then 5th Indian Brigade and tanks toward Tummar; 7th Armoured screened the southern flank and disrupted the coast road.
- The [Australian official history, *To Benghazi*, chapter 6](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Australia/Bengazi/Benghazi-6.html) gives the tighter sequence: Nibeiwa fell on the morning of **9 December**; the 7th RTR attacked Tummar West that afternoon; Tummar East was occupied on the morning of **10 December**; Sidi Barrani town fell at 4:40 p.m. on **10 December**, with isolated pockets surrendering on the 11th. This is the timing benchmark for the trial, rather than simply town ownership by the 11th.
- The UK official history says Wavell had decided by 11 December to send 4th Indian Division to Sudan. The Australian official history places the division's departure from the battlefield on **14 December** and says 16th British Brigade remained at Sidi Barrani. The trial therefore withdraws its 4th Indian HQ, 11th and 5th Indian Brigades and divisional artillery **on the 14 December turn**. Applying one date to all four counters is still an abstraction of formation movements.

## Existing map and deliberate abstractions

The map already contains Nibeiwa `(77,18)`, Sidi Barrani `(77,15)`, Maktila `(80,17)`, Buq Buq `(73,16)`, Sofafi `(73,20)`, **Bir Rabia `(74,20)`**, Mersa Matruh `(91,18)`, Bardia, Tobruk and the westward campaign area. The Sidi Barrani–Buq Buq road is connected. The trial sets Mersa Matruh as the Commonwealth railhead for 1940. No new map is needed for the opening.

The [UK official history, chapter XIV](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-I/UK-Med-I-14.html) calls the Nibeiwa–Rabia gap about fifteen miles in its plan discussion; [Wavell's despatch](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/LondonGazette/37628.pdf) describes the wider Sofafi–Nibeiwa opening as over twenty miles. These describe different endpoints and establish a usable opening, but neither text fixes the pass to one 10 km hexside. The [Australian War Memorial catalog](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/LIB100044808) identifies a 1:100,000 Sidi Barrani West sheet derived from a 1939 survey and revised in 1941; that sheet is a candidate for the local terrain audit, with its later revisions kept in mind. The shared map barrier remains unchanged pending a direct plate comparison and a two-sided gameplay check.

Tummar West and East have no named places. The trial uses an adjacent `(78,17)` camp sector, without claiming an exact historical camp coordinate. Individual Italian camp garrisons, British brigade strengths, stockpiles, lift, sea throughput and air availability are game estimates. The Italian HQ at Sidi Barrani represents the **Group of Libyan Divisions** named in the Australian official history; its precise hex remains an estimate, and it should not be presented as the Tenth Army HQ. The low initial Italian camp cohesion and green experience represent surprise and slow response; they are not archival readiness figures. The temporary link from 16th British Brigade to Western Desert Force HQ is a command/supply model approximation that keeps it active when 4th Indian HQ leaves. The Nibeiwa order is `reach`: the assault force can continue north after breaking the camp, while holding Sidi Barrani and blocking the coast road remain end-state orders.

The concealed approach on 7–8 December, rear entrance to Nibeiwa, mines, tank/infantry assault method and staggered hours **within 9 December** are not reproduced by this trial. A daily turn allows separate orders on 9, 10 and 11 December, but the same 7th RTR counter cannot assault Nibeiwa and Tummar in one day as it did historically. The initial plan in the official histories was to destroy the Nibeiwa–Tummar camps first, screen the Sofafi flank, and exploit north and west if successful; Sidi Barrani was a following phase. The draft's single end-state mission compresses these orders.

## Trial checks and finding

The scenario checker passes and every starting unit traces supply after the first supply phase. At seed `1940`, eight daily turns completed with game invariants intact. In Hard/Hard play, Nibeiwa fell on 9 December and Sidi Barrani first fell on **11 December**, one day later than the town's historical fall; the Indian counters remained through that turn and withdrew on **14 December**. In Standard/Standard play, Sidi Barrani first fell on **12 December**, two days late. Passive/passive play preserved the Axis camps. The Axis unit assigned to the Tummar sector fell back into Sidi Barrani on 10 December in these computer games; its fate and the detailed attack sequence still differ from the official account. These are diagnostic runs, not balance statistics or proof of historical accuracy.

The daily turn removes the former two-day scheduling obstacle. It does not reproduce the two assaults on 9 December by the same tank regiment. The opt-in trial lets a human assess that abstraction, the order of battle, and the supply estimates without presenting the chapter as historically verified. Lowering arbitrary garrison figures was rejected as a way to force an outcome.

### Opening-turn trace and map experiment

Run `.venv/bin/python scripts/trace_compass.py --axis hard --cw hard --seed 1940 --turns 3` to reproduce the first three daily decisions and battles. On 9 December the computer captures Nibeiwa, but sends 4th Armoured Brigade toward Sofafi instead of cutting the coast road. On the 10th its tanks engage the Tummar-sector counter while 11th Indian, 5th Indian and 16th British Brigades continue to fight the retreating Maletti group. The 2nd Libyan counter retreats into Sidi Barrani instead of being overcome at Tummar. The 7th RTR's strength barely falls across these attacks, whereas the official history reports a steep loss of **working** tanks that day. The 16th Brigade also cannot follow through Nibeiwa after the battle within the same turn: movement and combat each happen once, with enemy zones of control settled before the camp falls. These are model and planner discrepancies, not grounds for changing strengths to fit a date.

The [UK official history](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-I/UK-Med-I-14.html) describes a 15-mile Nibeiwa–Rabia gap and the [Australian account](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Australia/Bengazi/Benghazi-6.html) puts 4th Armoured Brigade across the Sidi Barrani–Buq Buq road on 9 December. The present map has a vehicle barrier west of Nibeiwa at `(75,18)`–`(75,17)`, making the brigade's modeled route pass through the crowded Allied concentration at `(76,18)`. I tested an approximate local crossing there **in memory and in a regenerated map, then restored the map**: it did allow vehicle movement but also let nearby Axis reserves intervene immediately. With the draft's present orders and combat rules, Hard/Hard lost Sidi Barrani and even Hard Commonwealth against passive Axis took until 13 December. A crossing alone is therefore not a validated correction. The exact 10 km crossing, Italian response timing and 1941 balance must be assessed together before changing the shared map.

## Next evidence and implementation gates

1. Validate per-formation and local garrison strengths, dates and positions with official records or mark them as estimates in the eventual player-facing scenario.
2. Investigate a general surprise/prepared-camp and response mechanism at this scale, including Matildas, perimeter entrances, flank screening, and the difference between tanks destroyed and tanks temporarily unserviceable. Check its effects on Crusader before using it for Compass.
3. Review daily movement, recovery and weather against the 9–10 December action. Fortification work now takes two days per level at either turn length; check its tactical effect. Test a bounded follow-through rule for reserves and successive attacks, with fuel, stores, cohesion and enemy reaction still charged, only if it improves other scenarios too.
4. Audit the Nibeiwa–Rabia gap and the Sidi Barrani slope at 10 km per hex against official map plates before changing a pass in the shared map. Repeat the AI trace with both sides; a traversable gap must not imply an instant historically impossible response.
5. Verify port/dump quantities, railhead, truck reach and the dated arrival of 6th Australian Division before adding Bardia and the pursuit chapters.
6. Only then run a human review and several seeded AI matches, validate mission outcomes and campaign continuity, and move the scenario into `data/scenarios/` and the campaign catalog.
