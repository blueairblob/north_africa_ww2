# Battle result presentation

The result panel follows the order of an operations report: orders, ground held, then losses and the commander's assessment. Contemporary reports describe the mission and what changed on the ground before giving a judgment. The U.S. Army's [II Corps operations report](https://history.army.mil/Portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/23-12.pdf) has separate entries for orders, own and enemy action, result, and estimate of the situation. This is a model for the report's structure, not a source for the 1941 scenario.

For Sidi Rezegh, the scenario orders in `data/scenarios/sidi_rezegh.json` define a local objective: the Commonwealth must hold Sidi Rezegh and Belhamed to establish the modeled Tobruk link; the Axis must deny it. W. E. Murphy's [official New Zealand history, *The Relief of Tobruk*](https://22battalion.org.nz/publications/histories/tobruk.pdf), and the Australian War Memorial's [official history chapter on Ed Duda](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1417191), inform the historical setting. The game report describes only the final ownership of its mapped positions. It must not imply that holding two scenario objectives proves a sustained real-world supply route or that any particular tactical manoeuvre occurred.

The victory grade remains the rules result: objective points plus points for enemy unit losses, measured against the scenario's starting-position baseline (`docs/RULES.md` §13.6). The field assessment is a separate plain-language account of the High Command aims and the final map state. A tactical points victory can coexist with a contested corridor; the report should say so. Losses listed under the Axis-view spectator report are the Axis formations known to that view.
