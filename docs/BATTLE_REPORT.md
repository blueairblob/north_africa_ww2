# Battle result presentation

The result panel follows the order of an operations report: orders, ground held, then losses and the commander's assessment. Contemporary reports describe the mission and what changed on the ground before giving a judgment. The U.S. Army's [II Corps operations report](https://history.army.mil/Portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/23-12.pdf) has separate entries for orders, own and enemy action, result, and estimate of the situation. This is a model for the report's structure, not a source for the 1941 scenario.

For Sidi Rezegh, `data/scenarios/sidi_rezegh.json` encodes the Commonwealth order to occupy Sidi Rezegh and Belhamed and clear a marked corridor to Tobruk; the Axis order is to block it. W. E. Murphy's [official New Zealand history, *The Relief of Tobruk*](https://22battalion.org.nz/publications/histories/tobruk.pdf), and the Australian War Memorial's [official history chapter on Ed Duda](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/C1417191), inform the historical setting. The route is an operational abstraction, since Ed Duda is not a named map place and Belhamed stands for the join. The report checks actual final unit positions and must not imply a sustained real-world supply route or a manoeuvre it cannot observe.

The victory grade follows `docs/RULES.md` §13.6: High Command orders decide when a scenario encodes them, with the points swing retained as secondary context. In Operation Crusader, failure to occupy Tobruk means at least an Axis major victory. In Sidi Rezegh, the relief corridor must actually be clear and both named places occupied; the Axis can win by blocking the corridor even when Commonwealth points improve. The field assessment lists orders met or missed so the judgment can be checked. Losses listed under the Axis-view spectator report are the Axis formations known to that view.
