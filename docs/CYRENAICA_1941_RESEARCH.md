# Cyrenaica defence and Axis build-up, February–March 1941

**Status, 7 November 2026:** `data/scenario_drafts/cyrenaica_preparation.json` is an eighth linked research chapter, covering **10 February–31 March 1941** in 25 two-day turns. It continues the played Beda Fomm state. No 1941 position is reset to the historical result, and it is not a standalone historical opening.

## High Command orders and dates

The [UK official history, *The Mediterranean and Middle East*, volume II, chapter I](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-II/UK-Med-2-1.html) says the Commonwealth intended to hold Cyrenaica with a reduced force while sending formations to Greece. It describes 7th Armoured as mechanically exhausted, 2nd Armoured as a weak, partly worn formation, and the 9th Australian Division as short of weapons, signals and transport. Wavell instructed Neame to delay an attack and preserve the force even if Benghazi had to be evacuated; keeping the port was secondary to avoiding a serious reverse. The chapter now requires the Commonwealth to keep at least **60% of the 2nd Armoured mobile force's actual entry steps** as well as to hold Tobruk. The percentage is a game threshold, not a historical order. It still cannot distinguish working tanks from temporarily unserviceable ones or judge the full Cyrenaica force.

The [Australian War Memorial Tobruk timeline](https://www.awm.gov.au/learn/schools/resources/tobruk-diaries/timeline) dates 9th Australian relief of 6th Australian to **8–9 March**, the Axis attack to **31 March**, and the Tobruk siege to **10–11 April**. The official history records Rommel's arrival in Tripoli on 12 February, German reconnaissance and anti-tank troops moving east during February, and German troops identified west of El Agheila on 24 February. It places the Panzer Regiment at El Agheila on 23 March, the Axis occupation of that place on 24 March, and the attack on Mersa Brega on 31 March. Those are dated anchors for model entries and objectives. They are not proof of the exact strength or position of each counter.

The chapter schedules the 7th Armoured counters to withdraw on a turn containing **20 February** and the 6th Australian counters on a turn containing **10 March**. Those are **game estimates** for counter-level withdrawal, informed by the broader refit and relief period. New 2nd Armoured and 9th Australian counters enter at Mersa Matruh so a counterfactual Axis-held Tobruk does not receive Commonwealth units inside its garrison. The 3rd Indian Motor Brigade is due at El Adem on 29 March in the official history; its counter strength is estimated. If El Adem is enemy-held, the entry falls back to a friendly base rather than spawning a unit behind enemy lines. Axis DAK units enter at an unlabelled west-edge hex outside El Agheila so they can still arrive if the Commonwealth holds the town; their modelled entry dates do **not** purport to date arrival at Tripoli. Ariete's 60-tank counter and map entry are also provisional.

## Model limitations before promotion

- The chapter's Commonwealth mission checks physical occupation of Tobruk and the surviving step percentage of 2nd Armoured's mobile force from the chapter-entry state. The game still lacks a separate mechanical-serviceability pool and does not score the wider delaying action or every formation's survival.
- A dated `port_access` event now stops Commonwealth landings at Benghazi from 18 February while preserving stock already ashore and leaving other ports open. The April chapter carries that closure forward. This is an aggregate landing rule; it does not simulate mines, air attack, unloading damage or a later safe reopening.
- Armoured serviceability, desert transport, wireless shortages and prepared depot locations are not individually modelled. The new strengths and entry hexes are research estimates.
- Two-day turns place a dated arrival within the turn containing its date. The game does not distinguish 23 March morning from 22 March on that turn.
- A Hard/Hard trace establishes structural continuity and engine invariants only. It cannot validate the historical outcome when the preceding research chapters leave Bardia and Tobruk Axis-held.

Before release, build a separately sourced 10 February opening, test plausible played states where the Commonwealth *did* take Benghazi, and validate the linked April reversal without teleporting either side. The [official history's chapter II](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-II/UK-Med-2-2.html) is the starting source for that review.

## Linked 1–11 April reversal

`data/scenario_drafts/cyrenaica_reversal.json` now continues the actual 31 March state through 11 April with daily turns. The [official history's chapter II](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-Med-II/UK-Med-2-2.html) records that the Commonwealth Support Group held Mersa Brega through **31 March** and withdrew afterward; the German force occupied it on **1 April**. The March chapter therefore orders the Axis to stage on its western road approach, while the April chapter orders the subsequent pursuit. The official account places German reconnaissance in Benghazi on 4 April, the Australian withdrawal on 6–7 April, and the 18th Australian Brigade at Tobruk by 7 April. The [AWM Tobruk timeline](https://www.awm.gov.au/learn/schools/resources/tobruk-diaries/timeline) dates the siege's onset to 10–11 April.

The April chapter gives the Commonwealth a physical **hold Tobruk** order. Axis orders mark Benghazi, Mechili and an eastern Tobruk road approach. That last hex is a **map-scale cue, not a siege test**: no explicit blockade, naval resupply, encirclement or garrison endurance rule exists yet. An optional commendation recognises preservation of at least 60% of the 9th Australian infantry counters' actual 1 April strength; this threshold is a game estimate and has no effect on victory. Brescia and 18th Australian counters have estimated strengths; the latter enters at Tobruk only when that port is friendly, otherwise it falls back to a friendly base. The Hard/Hard seed `1940` nine-chapter trace passes invariants and ends with Commonwealth control of Tobruk after an Axis-held March position. This is an alternate played outcome and shows that the chapter does not force the historical result. It does not establish balance or historical accuracy.

## Linked 12 April–4 May siege opening

`data/scenario_drafts/tobruk_siege_opening.json` continues the played 11 April state for 23 daily turns. The [Australian War Memorial Tobruk timeline](https://www.awm.gov.au/learn/schools/resources/tobruk-diaries/timeline) dates the repelled Easter Battle to **13–14 April** and the Battle of the Salient to **30 April–4 May**. Its [Tobruk account](https://www.awm.gov.au/collection/E84833) says the 9th Australian Division was ordered to hold Tobruk, deny its port to the Axis and delay the advance while frontier defences were prepared. The trial gives the Commonwealth a hold-Tobruk order and the Axis a take-Tobruk aim, judged from the played state. The old map and unit roster are placeholders for continuity; no historical 12 April deployment has been verified. The model has no detailed outer perimeter, captured-post scale, siege pressure or timed attack plan, so this chapter cannot yet reproduce either historical battle. It keeps Commonwealth sea access to friendly Tobruk, as the historical garrison was resupplied by sea.
